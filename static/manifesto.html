The Attestation Manifesto

Every AI call deserves
an economic receipt.

Every transaction in the financial system has a record. Every credit card swipe, wire transfer, and stock trade is logged, attested, and auditable. AI doesn't have this. We're building it.

The Problem No One Is Solving

Companies are spending millions on AI with no financial record of what that money produced. They can tell you their cloud bill. They can tell you their headcount cost. But ask them: "What did your AI spend produce for each customer, and can you prove it?" Silence.

This isn't a monitoring problem. Observability tools tell you what happened technically. This is an economic attestation problem. There is no infrastructure that records what AI costs, what it produces, and whether the unit economics work. Per customer. Per feature. Per dollar of revenue.

Per-token costs are falling. But total costs per task are rising. As reasoning models consume 10–100x more tokens, companies are spending more on AI than ever — with less visibility into what it's worth.

What Attestation Means

An attestation is a cryptographically sealed record of an economic event. When your AI makes a call, the Finault Seal captures: what model was used, what it cost, what it produced, and who it was for. That record is hashed, signed, and chained to every other record in your organization. Immutable. Verifiable. Auditable.

This is not logging. Logs are mutable, scattered, and exist for debugging. Attestations are permanent economic records that exist for governance, compliance, and business intelligence.

Why This Matters Now

AI pricing is shifting from tokens to outcomes. When you charge customers $49/month for "unlimited AI features," you need to know your cost-to-serve per customer. When a customer comes up for renewal, you need to prove your margins support your pricing. When your board asks "are our AI features profitable?", you need a number, not a guess.

The EU AI Act requires documentation retention for 10 years. Colorado's AI Act mandates annual impact assessments. SOC 2 auditors are asking about AI governance controls. The regulatory wave is coming. The companies that have attestation infrastructure will be ready. The rest will be scrambling.

The companies that win in AI won't just build the best models. They'll be the ones who can prove their AI economics work — to customers, investors, regulators, and their own board.
