The Scale

AI spending is growing faster than any technology category in history.

In a single quarter (Q2 2025), organizations spent $82 billion on compute and storage hardware for AI deployments — a 166% year-over-year increase. The AI infrastructure market is on track to reach $758 billion by 2029.

At the enterprise level, AI now accounts for 30% of overall IT budget increases despite total IT budgets rising only 1.8%. The average monthly enterprise spend on AI rose to $85,521 in 2025, with the proportion investing over $100,000 per month more than doubling from 20% to 45% in a single year. IDC, ISG, CloudZero (n=500)

The Forecasting Crisis

Enterprises are spending billions they can't predict.

The most alarming finding across all research is the near-total inability of enterprises to forecast AI costs. Only 15% can forecast within ±10%. Fully 80% miss forecasts by more than 25%, and nearly a quarter are off by 50% or more. Mavvrik / Benchmarkit (n=372)

The root cause: AI introduces fundamentally new cost units — tokens, GPU hours, inference jobs — where minor usage changes can spike spend by 100x compared to traditional compute. Unlike predictable SaaS licensing, consumption-based AI pricing creates volatile cost profiles that existing budgeting tools can't model.

Hidden Cost Drivers

Tokens aren't the problem. Everything else is.

One of the most counterintuitive findings: token costs are not the primary driver of AI spending. Data platform usage (56%), network access (49%), GPU charges (45%), and storage (41%) all outrank token costs, which came in fifth at 37%. Organizations solely tracking token usage are governing less than half the actual AI cost surface. Mavvrik / Benchmarkit (n=372)

GPU cloud prices have seen dramatic volatility — AWS H100 instances dropped from ~$7/hour to $3.90 after a 44% price cut in June 2025, while output tokens remain 3–8x more expensive than input tokens across providers. True cost governance must span compute, storage, APIs, data pipelines, network traffic, and specialized hardware.

Shadow AI

The fastest-growing financial blind spot in the enterprise.

Up to 58% of employees use AI productivity tools daily, with almost half uploading sensitive company data to unauthorized platforms. Spending on AI-native applications rose 75% in 2024, averaging $398,271 per organization. Shadow AI incidents increase the cost of a data breach by $670,000 per incident. The Regulatory Tsunami

Compliance deadlines are compressing. The cost of inaction is existential.

The EU AI Act entered enforcement in phases starting February 2025, with high-risk AI system requirements hitting August 2026. Penalties reach up to 7% of global revenue. Over 550 AI-related bills have been introduced across 45+ U.S. states, and 65+ nations have published national AI strategies. EU AI Act, Stanford AI Index 2025

Compliance costs are significant: large enterprises face $8–15 million for high-risk system compliance; mid-size companies, $2–5 million initially. The AI governance market is projected to reach $7.4 billion by 2030 at a 51% CAGR — reflecting the shift from voluntary oversight to mandatory regulatory infrastructure. NMSC, Greenberg Traurig

The Governance Gap

Everyone has AI in production. Almost no one governs it.

80% of enterprises have 50+ GenAI use cases in pipeline, but governance remains critically immature. Only 1% believe they've reached AI maturity. Only 1.5% of organizations believe they have adequate governance headcount. 80% report no tangible EBIT impact from GenAI. McKinsey, IAPP, ModelOp AI Governance Benchmark

The Market Opportunity

Three markets are converging into one.

Cloud FinOps ($14.9B → $26.9B by 2030), AI governance ($940M → $7.4B by 2030), and enterprise financial controls are converging into a single discipline: AI financial governance. No existing player occupies the intersection. MarketsandMarkets, NMSC, Grand View Research

Traditional FinOps tools (CloudZero, Datadog, Flexera) focus on cloud cost optimization but lack AI-specific cost attribution, compliance frameworks, and ERP integration. AI governance platforms (ModelOp, Credo AI, IBM) focus on model risk and ethics but can't answer the CFO's fundamental question: what did we spend on AI, and what value did it create?

No platform currently combines invoice parsing, inference-level cost attribution, cryptographic audit trails, FOCUS 1.3 compliance, and bidirectional ERP integration. The most mature organizations are building this capability in-house; the rest are waiting for the category to form.
